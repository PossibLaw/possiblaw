# IP Assignment Agreement — Required Fact Template

Use this table when the matter type is an IP assignment agreement (standalone, M&A schedule, employee/contractor assignment, or recordation-driven assignment). Several fields here are jurisdiction-sensitive — especially California Labor Code §2870 for employee assignments — and recordation requirements vary by IP type.

| Field | Why it matters | Acceptable inputs | Default if any |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Assignor legal name | The party giving up rights must be identified exactly; misnaming defeats chain of title. | Full registered legal name (or full legal name for individuals) | NO DEFAULT |
| Assignor entity type | Determines signature authority and capacity to assign. | e.g. `Delaware C-corporation`, `California LLC`, `individual` | NO DEFAULT |
| Assignor jurisdiction | Affects authority, marital-property issues for individuals, and choice-of-law analysis. | State, country | NO DEFAULT |
| Assignee legal name | Same as assignor; required for clean chain of title and recordation. | Full registered legal name | NO DEFAULT |
| Assignee entity type | Same as assignor. | e.g. `Delaware C-corporation` | NO DEFAULT |
| Assignee jurisdiction | Same as assignor. | State, country | NO DEFAULT |
| IP subject matter | Different IP types require different assignment language and recordation steps. | One or more of: `patents`, `patent applications`, `trademarks`, `trademark applications`, `copyrights`, `mask works`, `trade secrets`, `know-how`, `domain names`, `social-media handles`, `general — all IP` | NO DEFAULT |
| Description or schedule of IP | Without specific identification, the assignment may be void for vagueness, especially for patents (application numbers) and registered marks (registration numbers). | Schedule listing patent numbers / application numbers / registration numbers / titles / filing dates / countries, or a clear definitional scope for unregistered rights | NO DEFAULT for registered IP; for general assignments, a defined-term scope is acceptable |
| Consideration | Required for enforceability in most US jurisdictions; the form matters for tax treatment and for whether the assignment is recordable as supported. | `lump sum` (state amount and currency), `royalty` (state rate and base), `equity` (state shares), `employment-related — included in employment compensation`, `nominal — $1 and other good and valuable consideration` | `nominal — $1 and other good and valuable consideration` ONLY for intra-group assignments where independent consideration is documented elsewhere; NO DEFAULT otherwise |
| Effective date | Sets when title transfers and starts statute-of-limitations clocks for warranties. | ISO date `YYYY-MM-DD` | Date of full execution |
| Recordation requirements | Patents and trademarks should be recorded with USPTO to perfect against subsequent purchasers; copyrights may be recorded with the US Copyright Office; foreign rights require local recordation. | List of recordation actions and responsible party (e.g. `USPTO assignment recordation — assignee responsibility within 90 days`) | For US-registered patents and trademarks: `USPTO recordation by assignee within 90 days`; copyrights: `optional` |
| Warranties of title | Standard market warranty that the assignor owns the IP and has the right to assign; absence shifts risk to the assignee. | `full warranty`, `limited warranty` (specify carve-outs), `as-is — no warranty` | `full warranty` |
| Warranties of non-infringement | Allocates risk that the assigned IP infringes a third party's rights; heavily negotiated and often qualified by knowledge. | `unqualified`, `to assignor's knowledge`, `excluded` | `to assignor's knowledge` |
| Further assurances | Obligates assignor to sign additional documents needed to perfect or record the assignment (especially important for foreign filings). | `included` or `excluded` | `included` |
| Power of attorney | Lets the assignee sign in the assignor's name to record or perfect; commonly included for employee assignments and irrevocable in many forms. | `included — limited to recordation`, `included — broad`, `excluded` | `included — limited to recordation` |
| Moral rights waiver | Where moral rights exist (copyright in most non-US jurisdictions, narrow contexts in the US such as VARA for visual art), they must be waived to the extent permitted; some jurisdictions (France, Germany) do not permit full waiver. | `waived to extent permitted by law`, `not addressed` | `waived to extent permitted by law` |
| Prior-work exclusion (Schedule A) | For employee or contractor assignments, lets the assignor carve out IP owned before engagement; protects both parties from disputes over background IP. | List of pre-existing works, or `none` (signed acknowledgement) | Include the schedule mechanism even if currently empty |
| California Labor Code §2870 carve-out | Required for employee assignments where the employee is California-based: the assignment cannot reach inventions developed entirely on the employee's own time, without employer equipment/supplies/facilities/trade-secrets, and not related to the employer's business or actual or demonstrably anticipated R&D. The required notice must be provided. | `included with §2870 notice`, `not applicable — non-California employee`, `not applicable — not an employee assignment` | `included with §2870 notice` whenever the assignor is a California-based individual employee |
| Similar state carve-outs | Delaware, Illinois, Kansas, Minnesota, North Carolina, Utah, Washington, and others have analogous employee-invention statutes; the language and required notice differ. | Identify applicable state and include conforming carve-out language | Include the conforming carve-out whenever the assignor's work state matches one of these jurisdictions |
| Governing law | Drives interpretation and remedies; for employee assignments, governing-law choice does not override mandatory employee-invention statutes of the work state. | US state or country | `Delaware` for inter-corporate assignments; the employee's work state for employee assignments |
| Venue / exclusive forum | Determines where disputes are litigated. | Court system and seat | Matches governing law |
| Dispute resolution | Some assignments use arbitration with carve-outs for injunctive IP relief; choice affects speed and confidentiality. | `litigation`, `arbitration with IP injunctive carve-out`, `arbitration` | `litigation` |
| Signature authority | The signer must have actual authority to assign on behalf of the assignor entity; for individuals, spousal consent may be required in community-property states for certain pre-marital or marital IP. | Name and title of signatory; for individuals in community-property states, note whether spousal consent is needed | NO DEFAULT |
